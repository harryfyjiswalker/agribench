EarthShift field delineation: segmentation models trained on one region or year (in-distribution, ID) and tested on another (out-of-distribution, OOD), following the EarthShift paper's tasks and metrics. Scores are mIoU in percentage points, mean over seeds.

PLANTEUR crop-type segmentation in France's tropical overseas territories: per-pixel heads on frozen representations, scored by mIoU over the 8 classes (Background included, void excluded), in percent. Which representation ranks fields best in an unseen year, region or country?

The correlation between predicted and observed field yield within each held-out year (leave one year out), region or country: how well a model puts the fields in the right order, whatever it gets wrong about the overall level, which no imagery-only model can know for a new year. Each point is the median over held-out groups with at least 10 fields, then the mean over subsets (crops for leave one country out); hollow points don't cover every subset yet, and only complete results set the order. 1 = perfect order, 0 = no better than random.

Does the winner win every year?

Rank persistence. In each subset, every input × head with a score in every held-out group is ranked by its field R² in each group (1 = best). Ranks are scaled to 0 (best) – 1 (worst) so subsets with different numbers of candidates can be averaged; the table shows each input's best head. A low mean rank with a low spread is a dependable choice; a low mean with a high spread wins on average but not reliably. Worst group: the lowest R² in any held-out group, averaged over subsets. Robustness table

Δ = ID − OOD (lower is better). ρ is effective robustness: the OOD score minus what the paper's fitted ID-to-OOD line predicts for that ID score (higher is better; 0 means as robust as the paper's models on average). Boundary IoU is measured at native 256 × 256 resolution. Learning rates were chosen on ID validation data only.
